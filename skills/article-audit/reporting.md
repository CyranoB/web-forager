# Audit report

Use an inverted pyramid: put the information that most affects the reader's judgment
first, then provide accountable claim-level evidence and supporting detail. Make the
opening summary and verdict useful on their own without stripping necessary caveats
from the sections below.

The default full audit is long-form because every scoped claim needs an accountable
verdict. Keep it dense: reuse the numbered claims from the initial scope, and give each
claim only its verdict, concise rationale, and decisive supporting and challenging
evidence. Keep source-by-source notes in the ledger unless the user requests an expanded
audit.

### Writing style

Write as a clear evidence brief. After the neutral article summary, lead each evaluative
section with the finding, then show the evidence and the qualification. Use plain,
concrete words; short sentences and paragraphs; and active voice when the actor is
known. Name the actor, source, and date instead of using vague attributions. Repeat the
exact term when precision matters. Keep necessary technical terms and define them
briefly on first use.

Make every sentence earn its place. Cut throat-clearing, generic transitions, clichés,
promotional adjectives, rhetorical questions, dramatic fragments, and invented labels.
Use periods or commas instead of em dashes. Vary sentence length naturally without
forcing parallel phrasing or groups of three. Use sentence-case headings and reserve
bold text for verdicts and information that materially helps scanning.

Keep facts, inference, and judgment distinct, but do not repeat the same point in each
section. Preserve material caveats while cutting prose. Prefer one concrete comparison
or number over an abstract claim. Put the strongest evidenced reason for the framing
judgment immediately after the bottom line.

Before sending, perform one clarity pass: cut words that add no meaning, replace vague
pronouns and abstract nouns, break overloaded sentences, and confirm that every factual
statement has a nearby source or is clearly marked as inference.

**Complete when:** a reader can skim the article summary, bottom line, and decisive
reasons; trace every important claim to evidence; distinguish fact from inference; and
understand the audit without specialist language that the subject does not require.

Write in the user's language and use this structure:

1. **Article in brief:** two or three neutral sentences stating the article's subject,
   thesis, and intended takeaway. Summarize what it says without previewing the audit
   verdict or repeating the opening paragraph.
2. **Bottom line:** two or three sentences giving the factual-reliability judgment,
   framing judgment, and confidence. State material limitations that could change the
   result.
3. **Why this is the judgment:** rank the two to four findings, omissions, or misleading
   comparisons that most affect the verdict. Keep each reason short and cite its
   decisive evidence nearby.
4. **Claims audited:** number each atomic claim, put its unchanged verdict label on the
   next line, then give the concise rationale, supporting evidence, and contradicting
   evidence or `none found after search`. For a material quotation, cite the earliest
   source found and state any surrounding context or wording difference that affects
   the verdict. Use this pattern:

   ```text
   1. Claim text
      **VERDICT**
      Rationale and decisive evidence.
   ```

5. **Missing voices and interests:** include omitted perspectives and sourced interests
   only when they could materially change the reader's judgment.
6. **Numbers in perspective:** show the denominators, base rates, comparisons,
   precedents, uncertainty, and forecast horizons that change how the article's numbers
   or predictions should be read.
7. **Limits and sources:** state unresolved evidence gaps, including documented
   quotation-tracing failures, and temporal limits. Then give a short, deduplicated
   source list with links annotated as support, challenge, baseline, or context. Cite
   material interests without repeating claim rationales.

Separate sourced findings from inference. State when a search produced no independent
evidence, and keep confidence proportional to the evidence.

**Complete when:** the report accounts for every scoped claim, both search directions,
every material missing or interested voice, the numerical context and uncertainty that
could change the reading, every material quotation's provenance or documented trace
gap, every unresolved limit, and every source's role. Include expanded research notes
only when they change the judgment or the user asks for them.
